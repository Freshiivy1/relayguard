How RelayGuard works

The threat. A scammer (or a voice bot) can place a call and play it through a nearby speakerphone, relaying audio from another room, another country, or a text-to-speech system. The far-end channel betrays this: bandwidth limits, codec cascades, loudspeaker distortion, room reverb and double-talk patterns that a normal handset call does not have.

The three-state verdict

Every call is windowed (2 s windows, 1 s hop) and fused into a single score, smoothed over time and compared against two calibrated thresholds:

  • GREEN — score below θgreen: the call looks like a normal direct call. Pass it through.
  • CHALLENGE — between the thresholds: suspicious but not proven. Verify the caller instead of dropping the call.
  • RED — score above θred: speakerphone relay detected with high confidence. Flag or terminate.

Thresholds are calibrated on a held-out dev set (Neyman–Pearson: high relay recall at a controlled false-positive rate).

The three evidence flags

  • Channel — what the audio path itself reveals. Five window-level detectors (a CNN on log-mel spectrograms, a gradient-boosted model on handcrafted telephony features, and bandwidth / reverb / distortion forensics) plus call-level channel-switch detection catch the acoustic fingerprint of a loudspeaker relay — including a switch onto one mid-call.
  • Identity — who is speaking. When voice biometrics are installed, a speaker voiceprint can be enrolled and a liveness challenge verifies the responder is the enrolled human, not a replayed or synthesized voice.
  • Behavior — how the call behaves. Conversation-coupling, background-scene tagging and replay/quantization cues look for uncoupled rooms, TTS artifacts and other patterns that accompany relayed or automated calls.

A learned, calibrated fuser combines all of this evidence. Guardrails void evidence that matches known benign confounders (car kits, headsets, aggressive noise suppression, uncoupled background TV).

Challenge, not terminate

CHALLENGE is a second chance, not an accusation: the philosophy is to challenge a suspicious call, not terminate it. The service speaks five random digits with a short time-to-live; a live human next to the phone can simply repeat them, while a replayed or piped-through bot typically fails the liveness, content, net-speech or identity check. Two retries are allowed before rejection, so a momentary bad line does not cut off a legitimate caller.

Current limitations

  • Speaker identity and neural anti-spoofing are optional modules: without them the API reports degraded: true or identity: unavailable rather than failing, and challenge verdicts rely on liveness and content checks alone.
  • The models are trained on telephony-band audio (16 kHz mono); very short clips, non-speech audio or heavy background noise reduce reliability.
  • Learning mode fine-tunes on your uploads plus a small anchor rehearsal set; with only a handful of samples, before/after metrics are in-sample estimates, not guarantees.
  • Determined attackers with high-quality speaker rigs in quiet rooms remain hard to catch — thresholds trade recall against false positives deliberately.
